Why I built this

The MCAT is expensive before you even sit for it. The big test-prep companies charge hundreds — sometimes thousands — of dollars for question banks and full-length simulators, and that cost quietly filters who gets to prepare well. I kept meeting premeds who were smart and driven but couldn't justify the price tag, so they made do with scattered PDFs and free-trial scraps.

I'm a phage biology PhD candidate, not a career software engineer — but I build tools for people I care about, increasingly with AI helping me move faster. Project 528 is that: a genuinely free, private MCAT prep tool that behaves like the paid ones, with the studying, timing, and analytics that actually move a score, minus the account and the invoice.

Everything runs locally in your browser. There's no login and no server collecting your progress — your practice history lives on your machine, and you can back it up or wipe it whenever you want.

What it does

⏱️

Full-length simulator

Mirrors the real AAMC structure: three 59-question science/social sections at 95 minutes and a 53-question CARS section at 90 — 230 questions over 375 content minutes.

📚

100k-question bank

A searchable bank of 100,000 bundled, original concept-generated questions across every MCAT section, with a live-filtered preview so browsing stays fast.

🔁

Spaced repetition

Dedicated modes for learning, practice, timed sets, mistakes review, and spaced repetition, so the questions you miss come back at the right time.

📊

Honest analytics

A dashboard tracking progress, streaks, a readiness estimate, weak topics, recent activity, and per-section progress — so you study what's actually weak.

✍️

Question editor

Write your own items with schema validation, import from JSON, export to CSV, and save custom questions into your personal bank.

🛡️

Safe content ingestion

Turn lawful source material — your notes, open-license text, public-domain content — into original draft questions, kept separate from the approved bank until you review them.

💾

Local persistence

Your progress is saved in the browser with backup, restore, progress reset, and full reset — you own your data and can move or erase it anytime.

🌗

Light & dark themes

A clean, keyboard-friendly interface with light and dark modes for long study sessions that don't fry your eyes.

How it works

  1. Open it in your browser

    Go to the app — no account, no install, no download. The full sharded question bank loads straight from the page.

  2. Pick a mode

    Learn a topic, drill a practice set, run a timed section, or sit a full-length simulation with real MCAT section timing and structure.

  3. Review your mistakes

    Missed items flow into a mistakes review and spaced-repetition queue so you revisit weak concepts on a schedule instead of forgetting them.

  4. Watch your analytics

    The dashboard updates your readiness estimate, streak, and weak-topic map so each session targets what's actually holding your score back.

  5. Add your own content

    Optionally paste lawful source material or write your own questions, run the safety check, and save approved items into your personal bank.

Local-first, private, and legally careful

Two principles shaped this app. First, privacy: there's no account and no backend. Your study history lives in your own browser, you can back it up or reset it on demand, and nothing about your prep is sold, tracked, or uploaded.

Second, doing content the honest way. The bank is built from original concept templates, not copied from AAMC or commercial prep products. The ingestion workflow is designed to turn lawful material — your notes, open-license text, public-domain sources — into fresh questions, never to scrape or lightly paraphrase copyrighted banks. Every generated draft is checked against its source with a word-shingle similarity test, and high-overlap items are blocked from publishing before a human ever approves them. It's a safety aid, not a legal guarantee, so review always stays in human hands.
